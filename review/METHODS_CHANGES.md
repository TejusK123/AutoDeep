## D1. Final fit, early stopping and search space

- **Old text** (Methods, "Hyper Parameter Tuning"): "n_estimators: how many boosted trees to include in the model. Tuning of this hyperparameter is heavily dependent on the choice of eta. User's of AutoDeep with a lot of training data and compute capacity should expect low eta values (<= 10^-3) and high n_estimator values (>= 800) to optimize their model." ... "In all of our training runs, this value [max_depth] consistently stayed between 4 and 6." ... "If one has less access to compute, consider eta in (10^-3, 10^-1)." ... "Optuna itself uses both relational (Covariance Matrix Evolution) and independent (Tree Parzen Estimators) sampling methods to optimize the feature search space."
- **New text:** "Each hyperparameter configuration is scored by its mean weighted F1 over the same ten stratified 80/20 splits, with balanced sample weights so that the three classes count equally. The number of trees is not tuned: in each split, a stratified fifth of the training portion is held out, and boosting stops when the weighted multiclass log-loss on it has not improved for 50 rounds (at most 3,000 trees); the configuration's number of trees is the mean over the ten splits. The search covers max_depth 2-8, learning rate (eta) 0.005-0.3 (log-uniform), min_child_weight 1-4, gamma 0-1, L1 and L2 penalties, and row and column subsampling, with Optuna's Tree-structured Parzen Estimator sampler (seeded; the first ten proposals are random). The released model is the best configuration refit on all training loci with the same balanced weights."
- **Note:** before D1, the saved model was the best configuration refit on the first, unstratified 80% split, without the class weights used to score it (manual hyperparameters: a stratified 80% split); max_depth could only be 1-5 (the shipped model's 5 was the top of the range, so the paper's "between 4 and 6" could not happen); base_score was tuned although it has no effect on a softmax model; n_estimators was tuned (100-1000) together with learning rates as low as 10^-4; and an ASHA scheduler was configured that never stopped a trial, because each trial reported once. The Optuna sampler is TPE only (since A6; no CMA-ES). The shipped model predates D1; D1b retrains it after B8.
- **Note:** the reported metrics are those of the selected configuration on the splits used to select it, so they are slightly optimistic; C1's nested leave-one-species-out evaluation gives the unbiased estimate.
- **Check (19 Oct 2026, bundled training data, 10 tuning rounds, seed 42, Apple M4):** best weighted F1 over the ten splits 0.875 +/- 0.013 before D1 (max_depth 5, eta 0.028, 874 trees) and 0.872 +/- 0.015 after (max_depth 8, eta 0.006, 1,186 trees by early stopping), within noise. The ten trials ranged 0.762-0.875 before, when most of them used learning rates of 10^-4 to 10^-3 with too few trees, and 0.838-0.872 after. With early stopping each split trains on 64% of the data instead of 80%; the final model uses all of it. Running time 47 s before, 66 s after. The best depth sat at the top of the new range, but depths 5 and 6 scored 0.870; check the depth of the best configurations again in D1b's longer tuning run.

## B2. 5' homogeneity: unbiased Simpson index of the mature-arm 5' starts

- **Old text** (submitted manuscript, Methods, "5' Homogeneity"): "To do this, we considered only the top n sequenced permutations of a potential pre-miRNA mature strand (where n is proportional to the amount of permutations found), then we counted reads associated with the permutation with the highest read count and found the fraction over the chosen permutations that aligned in both position and base pair. The additional feature num loci tabulates the amount of permutations found."
- **New text:** "To do this, AutoDeep takes the reads that miRDeep2 assigns to the mature arm, those in its mature read count (reads that start at most 2 nt before and end at most 5 nt after the mature sequence), and computes the unbiased Simpson index of their 5' start positions, sum n_i(n_i - 1) / [N(N - 1)], where n_i is the number of reads starting at position i and N the number of mature-arm reads. This is the probability that two reads drawn at random from the mature arm start at the same nucleotide; it is 1 when all of them do. Reads are grouped by start position whatever their first nucleotide. Candidates with fewer than two mature-arm reads have no value. A separate feature counts the distinct reads aligned to the precursor."
- **Note:** before B2, the code divided the reads that start where the most abundant read starts, with the same first nucleotide, by every read that starts no more than 20 nt after it. When the mature is on the 3' arm, the star and loop reads start upstream and so entered the denominator: in the training data the most abundant read is on the 3' arm in 1,585 of 2,907 loci, and for those the median value was 0.929, against 0.985 for the same share counted on the mature arm only. The "top n permutations" the old text describes were computed but never used (the pypdf code that computed them went in B1). `num_locis` is unchanged. Neither text matched the code; the new text does.
- **Note:** `training_data_pre/` keeps the old values until B8 rebuilds it, and the shipped model was trained on them. Until D1 retrains it, the model sees values on a different scale: the Simpson index is lower than the dominant read's share for the same locus (about its square: a share of 0.95 gives about 0.90). With only this feature changed, the shipped model changes 88 of the 2,907 training labels (47 Candidate to falsepositive, 25 falsepositive to Candidate). The Results sentence reading a threshold off the trees ("if the 5' ends of reads align with each other less than 91% of the time...") refers to the old definition; take any such threshold from the retrained model (C4).

### For the paper (why the Simpson index; Tejus chose it on 18 Oct 2026)

`review/homogeneity_measures.py` computes all of the numbers below on the mature-arm reads of the 2,907 training loci (output in `review/homogeneity_measures.txt`).
- **Same information as the alternatives:** the Simpson index, the dominant read's share, Shannon entropy (with or without the Miller-Madow correction) and the mean distance from the commonest start rank the loci almost identically (Spearman >= 0.99). 1,105 loci (38%) have a single 5' start, which all of them score as perfectly homogeneous. Tree models use only the order of values, so the choice changes robustness and readability, not what the model can learn.
- **No depth bias:** when the reads are thinned at random to 10%, as with a tenfold smaller library, the Simpson index shifts by +0.005 SD on average (+0.018 SD for the 903 loci with fewer than 50 mature-arm reads). The dominant share shifts by +0.053 SD (+0.116), Shannon entropy by -0.109 SD (-0.187). The unbiased estimator corrects exactly for drawing a finite number of reads. This matters for comparing species and libraries of different depth (B7, C1).
- **Easy to state:** "the probability that two mature-arm reads start at the same nucleotide". Its inverse is the effective number of 5' ends (e.g. 1.1).
- **Why not normalised entropy:** entropy divided by log2 of the number of distinct starts measures how evenly the reads are spread over the starts that were observed, not how concentrated they are. Two clean starts 1 nt apart score 1, the same as reads scattered over 20 starts, and adding stray reads lowers the value (toy cases in the output).
- **Why the start position only:** Drosha and Dicer read sequence near their cleavage sites (Drosha: the basal UG and mismatched GHG motifs and the CNNC motif; Dicer: the GYM motif at the cleavage site), so a base change can shift where they cut. A shifted cut shows up as a different start position, which the index counts. A read with a different first base at the same position was still cut there; the first nucleotide matters afterwards, for Argonaute loading. Only 0.39% of mature-arm reads have a mismatched first base. Counting the first base as part of the start would change the index by more than 0.05 in 26 of 2,907 loci (Spearman 0.986). Check the references before citing: Auyeung et al. 2013 (Cell 152:844); Fang and Bartel 2015 (Mol Cell 60:131); Kwon et al. 2019 (Mol Cell 73:505); Lee, Kim and Kim 2023 (Nature 615:323).
- **Model:** on a scratch copy of `training_data_pre/` with only this feature recomputed, the reviewer's `reanalysis.py` gives XGBoost (all 18 features) an accuracy of 0.864 in random 80/20 splits (unchanged) and 0.818 leave-one-species-out (0.812 before), with the largest per-species changes in B. tabaci (+0.03) and P. xylostella (-0.02). These differences are within the noise of a single run; the gain from B2 is a definition that is correct for 3'-arm miRNAs and robust to depth, not accuracy. The feature's share of the model's gain rises slightly (0.090 to 0.095).
- **Caveat to state:** reads from a paralogue that align to the precursor with a mismatch at their first base count as starting at the same position. With few reads the index is unbiased but noisy; with fewer than two it is undefined (1 training locus, chrX_23311).

## B3. Signed 3' overhangs at both ends of the duplex

- **Old text:** TODO (Tejus): paste the sentences that define "3' Overhang Bot" and "3' Overhang Top" (Methods and Table 1).
- **New text:** "For each candidate, AutoDeep computes the 3' overhang at both ends of the mature-star duplex, at the base of the stem (Drosha end) and at the loop (Dicer end), from miRDeep2's output.mrd: the mature, the most abundant star read and the RNAfold structure of the precursor that miRDeep2 used. Only base pairs between the mature and the star are considered, since Drosha and Dicer remove the flanks and the loop. At each end, the overhang is the number of unpaired nucleotides beyond the terminal mature-star base pair on the strand whose 3' end lies there, minus the number on the strand whose 5' end lies there. Positive values are 3' overhangs (2 for canonical processing), 0 is a blunt end and negative values are 5' overhangs. Candidates without star reads have no overhang values, because miRDeep2's star is then its own prediction."
- **Note:** before B3, "Bot" was the unsigned difference between the unpaired 5' and 3' ends of AutoDeep's own fold of the precursor, and "Top" came from a padding heuristic; both were computed even without star reads. The training data in `training_data_pre/` keep those values until B8 rebuilds them, and the shipped model was trained on them, so it has to be retrained (D1) before the new definition is used for the paper's results.

### For the paper (why the duplex rule; Tejus chose it on 18 Oct 2026)

- **Definition in one sentence:** "the 3' overhang at each end of the mature-star duplex, from the terminal base pair between the two strands". The alternative considered, miRDeep2's rule, needs "2 plus the offset of the observed star from the star miRDeep2 expects for 2-nt overhangs". That reads as circular even though it is not, and a reviewer may read it that way.
- **Biology:** an overhang is a property of the cut duplex. Drosha and Dicer each leave a 2-nt 3' overhang on the mature-star duplex, and once the flanks and loop are cut off, pairs with them no longer exist. miRDeep2's rule anchors on fixed mature positions (the first and third-from-last nucleotides), which can pair with the loop; that is the cause in 5 of the 10 Pomonella loci where the rules differ. It also changes when the mature call flips between arms: miRDeep2 calls the mature as the most abundant read, and MirGeneDB lists both arms as mature for 45 human genes. The duplex rule gives the same value whichever strand is the mature.
- **Structure uncertainty:** both rules depend on the predicted structure where it is least reliable: the duplex ends, next to bulges, and where the stem meets the loop or flanks. The choice of rule changes 10 of 148 Pomonella values with star reads (about 7%), about the same as swapping miRDeep2's older RNAfold structure for ViennaRNA 2.7.0's (about 3-4% of the same values). Do not interpret a single locus's value. The duplex rule has fewer extreme values (MirGeneDB 3.0 human, 506 precursors whose fold pairs the two arms: 3 versus 10 beyond +/-5) and gives 2 at both ends slightly more often (378 versus 373).
- **Model:** the main gain of B3 is the sign: before, a 2-nt 5' overhang looked the same as the canonical 2-nt 3' overhang. The overhangs are minor features (about 5% of the shipped model's mean |SHAP|: Top 0.028, Bot 0.026), so the choice of rule should not change accuracy measurably. The duplex rule is less redundant with `miRDeep2_score`, whose star-read component uses miRDeep2's expected star. Report the C5 ablation: if removing both overhangs costs nothing, say so.
- **Caveat to state:** a frayed end, with unpaired nucleotides on both strands beyond the terminal pair, nets to 0, the same as a blunt end (e.g. QFTL02000009.1_39298: 5 and 5).
- **Loci where the rules differ** (C. pomonella; positions 1-based along the obs, pri_seq and pri_struct lines of output.mrd; "old" is the pre-B3 code on AutoDeep's own fold, unsigned; Drosha / Dicer):

  | Locus | Old | miRDeep2's rule | Duplex rule |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | QFTL02000135.1_4908 | 2 / 1 | 2 / -1 | 2 / 3 |
  | QFTL02000009.1_39390 | 2 / 2 | 2 / -2 | 2 / 2 |
  | QFTL02000001.1_671 | 6 / 5 | 6 / 5 | 6 / 6 |
  | QFTL02000004.1_17907 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 4 | 2 / 2 |
  | QFTL02000009.1_39298 | 3 / 0 | 4 / 5 | 4 / 0 |
  | QFTL02000009.1_39327 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 | 1 / 2 |
  | QFTL02000015.1_6015 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 1 |
  | QFTL02000447.1_20491 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 1 |
  | QFTL02000007.1_25533 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 | -3 / 2 |
  | QFTL02000020.1_10445 | 3 / 1 | 5 / 0 | 5 / -1 |

  In the first five, miRDeep2's anchor pairs with a loop nucleotide; in the last five it is unpaired, and miRDeep2's rule counts the unpaired nucleotides as paired.
